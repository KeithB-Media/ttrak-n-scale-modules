# T-TRAK N-scale City: 2 Double Modules + Printable Buildings (v2, "lit Main Street")

Everything is generated by **`ttrak_city.py`**, a parametric Blender script. Edit the numbers
at the top and run it again to regenerate the scene, all STLs and the renders.

## Run it
- **Blender GUI (4.5+ / 5.x):** open the *Scripting* workspace, then Open `ttrak_city.py`, then Run Script.
  It rebuilds only the `TTRAK` collection.
- **Headless:** `blender -b -P ttrak_city.py -- --no-render` (leave out `--no-render` to also produce the renders, which take about 5 minutes on CPU).

Output in `ttrak_output/`: `stl/buildings`, `stl/roads`, `parts_list.csv` (sizes, quantities, fits your bed),
`ttrak_city.blend` and the night renders.

## Module geometry (check these against your club's T-TRAK standard)
| Item | Value used |
|---|---|
| Double module | 618 W x 355 D mm |
| Track per line | 620 mm (1 mm overhang each end) = Kato 248 + 248 + 124 mm straights |
| Front edge to front-track roadbed edge | 38 mm |
| Mainline spacing | 33 mm centre to centre |
| Module top above table | 70 mm |

## Layout (front to back)
| y (mm) | What |
|---|---|
| 0–96 | Double track |
| 100–162 | Street tile: 3 mm curb strip, 47 mm road (~7.5 m), 12 mm sidewalk |
| 162–206 | **Row 1**: 2–4 storey shops (awnings, sign bands, theater marquee, hotel, corner turrets) |
| 210–266 | **Row 2**: continuous 5–6 storey apartment blocks with mansard roofs and dormers |
| 327–353 | **Row 3**: shallow (26 mm deep) high-rises for the skyline |

Module B adds a cross street with a crosswalk. Buildings stand on a 2 mm styrene/MDF city base
(not printed) that is flush with the sidewalks.

## Building library (`BUILDINGS`)
Each building is set by its width, depth, floor heights, and a **window style per floor**:
`shop` (storefront with mullions), `plain`, `frame`, `pediment`, `arch`, `hood`.
Each building can also have:
- awnings, sign band, marquee, blade sign, corner turret
- a textured (grooved) ground floor, dentil cornice, floor bands
- a flat roof with a water tank, AC unit, chimney or billboard, or a mansard roof.

Add a new variant in one line, for example `apt(58, "pink", ["shop","arch","frame","frame","hood"])`, then use it in `LAYOUT`.

## Printing (FDM, 0.4 mm nozzle; resin also works)
- **Shells print upright, with no supports.** Walls are 1.6 mm. Cornices, sills, hoods and pediments
  overhang 1.2 mm or less. Awnings, marquees, blade signs and the turret corbel are 45° wedges.
- **Windows are cut through** (`WINDOWS_THROUGH = True`) for LED lighting. To stop light
  bleeding through the walls, paint the inside black or silver, or print in an opaque colour. Glue clear
  acetate or frosted tape behind the windows. Side walls between buildings have no windows,
  so neighbouring buildings don't light each other.
- **Flat roofs** drop in behind the parapet and rest on the corner posts.
  **Mansard roofs** sit on top of the walls with a 2 mm locating plug. They are hollow, so the
  building's LEDs light the dormers. The ceiling inside is a short bridge (under 30 mm), so it prints without supports.
- Suggested settings: 0.12 mm layers, 15% infill, no supports, brim on the high-rises.
- Leave all roofs loose (not glued) so you can reach the LEDs.

## What to buy instead of print
Vehicles, figures, street lights (the render lamps only show where they go), and fire
escapes (etched brass). Fire escapes are too fine for FDM.

# Distribution-center warehouse (`warehouse.py`)
A generic modern fulfillment-center style warehouse: tilt-up concrete panels, dock doors,
a curtain-wall office, an accent band and a **blank sign panel for your own decal** (no logos are modelled).
Run `python warehouse.py` or `blender -b -P warehouse.py`; it needs `ttrak_city.py` in the same folder.
Output goes to `ttrak_output/warehouse/`.

| Part | Size (mm) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `wh_seg_left` | 156 x 152 x 84 | Office end: glass curtain wall, entry canopy, sign panel, 3 docks |
| `wh_seg_mid` | 156 x 142 x 84 | 5 docks. Print `MID_SEGMENTS` copies to make the building longer |
| `wh_seg_right` | 150 x 142 x 84 | Closed end, 5 docks |
| `wh_roof_*` | ~149 x 137 | Drop-in roofs with skylights, rooftop units and a hatch |
| `trailer_53ft_print_upside_down` | 101 x 17 x 26 | 53' dry van, already flipped so the roof sits on the bed |
| `delivery_van` | 38 x 12 x 17 | Generic high-roof cargo van |

- **Scale:** a real fulfillment center would be over 2 m long in N scale. This is a selectively
  compressed dock-wall section (450 x 140 mm, 12.8 m tall) that fits a T-TRAK double
  module with a truck court deep enough for 53' trailers.
- **Segments glue together:** each joint lines up with a panel joint, so the seam is hidden.
  Built-in tongues on the inside of the walls align the segments. Floor ties keep each segment square.
- **Prints with no supports:** shells print upright. The door seals, light hoods and entry canopy
  have short overhangs or bridges.
- **Painting:** concrete grey or white walls, a colour of your choice on the raised accent band
  and sign panel, dark grey or blue glass behind the mullions, and black dock seals and bumpers.

# Portal North Bridge module set (`portal_bridge.py`)
A model of the new Portal North Bridge over the Hackensack River (Amtrak / NJ Transit, Northeast Corridor,
opened March 2026). Run `python portal_bridge.py`; output goes to `ttrak_output/portal_bridge/`.

**The set is four T-TRAK double modules:** West approach viaduct | Arch 1 | Arch 2 | Arch 3.

| Prototype | Model |
|---|---|
| 3 x 400 ft network tied arches | 3 x 600 mm arches, one per double module (79% length) |
| Arch ~86 ft tall | 150 mm rise above the tie |
| ~50 ft wide | 90 mm clear between the arch ribs, two Kato Unitrack lines at T-TRAK spacing |
| 50 ft over the water | 52 mm under the tie (river 62 mm below track base). Compressed so the modules keep standard T-TRAK height |

## Parts per arch module (STLs in `stl/`)
| Part | Qty / arch | Print |
|---|---|---|
| `arch_rib_end_L`, `arch_rib_end_R` | 2 + 2 | Flat, hanger face down (208 x 145 x 3 mm) |
| `arch_rib_mid` | 2 | Flat (200 x 162 x 3 mm) |
| `splice_tie`, `splice_rib` | 4 + 4 | Flat. Glue on the outer face across each joint |
| `arch_strut` | 5 | Flat. Top lateral struts between the two ribs |
| `arch_deck_print_upside_down` | 3 | Upside down (floor beams up) |
| `pier_half_print_on_joint_face` | 2 | Lying on its flat joint face. Two halves form one pier at each module joint |

The approach module needs 3 x `approach_span_print_on_end` (print standing on end, with a brim) and 5 pier halves.
`portal_bridge_parts.csv` has the full counts.

## Assembly
1. **Build the module as a lowered-scene box:** 6 mm ply base, a resin or acrylic-gel river, low end rails, and a narrow
   bulkhead at each end that hides behind the pier and carries the deck end at track height.
2. **Lay a 20 x 3 mm aluminium flat bar** in the channel under the three deck sections. It bridges pier to pier
   and carries the train load, so the plastic arch only has to look right. Glue the deck sections onto it.
3. **Assemble each arch side** (end + mid + end) on a flat surface over a printed 1:1 drawing, then glue on
   the splice plates. Stand the sides on the deck edges, with the hanger face toward the tracks.
4. **Add the top struts**, then glue the pier halves to the end bulkheads.
5. **Lay the Unitrack** (248 + 248 + 124 mm per line) on the deck. It overhangs each module end by 1 mm, as on any
   T-TRAK module.

## Notes
- **Hangers** are 0.9 x 1.2 mm. They print well at 0.12 mm layers with a 0.4 mm nozzle; resin gives finer cables.
- **Catenary** (overhead electric wires) is not modelled. If you want working catenary, Sommerfeldt or Viessmann N-scale
  catenary can be attached to the struts.
- **Paint:** light grey steel; concrete-grey piers and deck.

## Laser-cut module boxes (`portal_laser.py`)
Based on the "Double 618 x 330 T Track Module" laser template: 5 mm ply, the same 618 x 330 footprint and
70.09 mm height, the same finger-joint positions, and the same colours (purple = cut, kerf OUT;
green = inside cut, kerf IN; red = engrave or delete). Run `python portal_laser.py` to make the SVGs in
`ttrak_output/portal_bridge/laser/`:
- `portal_arch_module_618x330.svg`: cut **3** (one per arch)
- `portal_approach_module_618x330.svg`: cut **1**

| Part | Notes |
|---|---|
| Base plate | River bed. Its notches take the bottom tabs of every other part. 9 mm holes take a T-nut + leveling bolt at each corner, the same hole size as the template's leg brackets |
| Back apron | Full 70 mm height (backdrop), same end joints as the template |
| Front apron (arch) | A low 16 mm lip, so the river, piers and arches show from the front |
| Pier end plate (x2) | Hammerhead pier profile. **The plywood is the pier core**; glue the printed 4 mm `pier_face` on its inside face. The arches bear on the pier cap |
| Back end plate (x2) | Back corner of each end. The river runs open between the pier and back end plates |
| Approach only | Short top plate (0–80 mm) + standard template-style end plate, so the left end joins any normal T-TRAK module at track height. The abutment plate slots through both aprons and the base. Glue the printed `abutment_seat` to its face for the first viaduct span |

All parts were checked in 3D: the boxes assemble to exactly 618 x 330 x 70.09 mm, no parts overlap,
and every notch is filled by a tab.

**Water:** the river is a 3 mm pour on the base plate (resin or acrylic water). Tape a temporary dam across
the open river at each end while it cures.

**Level the modules** with the four corner bolts, from below.

# River-gap deck-girder bridge: 2 fully printed double modules (`river_bridge.py`)
A double-track steel **deck plate girder bridge** on massive flat-faced concrete piers over a rocky river,
with a wooded rock cliff behind it. Inspired by a classic eastern river-gap crossing. No real signs, logos or trains are modelled.
Everything, including the module bodies, is 3D printed.

Run `python river_bridge.py` (needs `pip install numpy manifold3d trimesh`; the renders also need `pip install bpy`).
Use `--no-render` to skip the renders. Output goes to `ttrak_output/river_bridge/`.

```
 Module A (West)                                 Module B (East)
 | std end | transition |   dropped river section   ||   dropped river section   | transition | std end |
 |  track  | embankment + abutment | pier | pier | joint pier | pier | pier | abutment + embankment |  track  |
```

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Module | 618 x 355 mm double, 70 mm to the track base, Kato Unitrack 248 + 248 + 124 per line, 38 mm / 33 mm track positions (same as the rest of this repo) |
| Transition (each module's outer end) | 40 mm at full height (joins any standard T-TRAK module), then the track runs on an embankment between concrete wing walls to an abutment at 142 mm. The ground drops away in front and behind. The back cliff fades to track level at the outer end |
| Dropped section | River bed 62 mm below the track base (8 mm above the table), 3 mm water pour to -59 mm. The cliff behind rises to 42 mm above the track |
| Spans | 3 per track per module, 146 mm long (~77 ft), 16 mm deep plate girders, 125 mm clear between piers |
| Piers | 40 x 100 mm under the coping, battered 2 mm per side, concrete lift lines. One split pier sits on the module joint, so each module keeps its own half and the modules separate normally |

Module B is a mirror of A, but the rock texture and the river rocks use world coordinates, so the two modules look different and the cliff and river line up across the joint.

## Printed parts (`stl/`, full list in `river_bridge_parts.csv`)
| Part | Qty (pair) | Size (mm) | Print |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A_tile_*`, `B_tile_*` | 6 + 6 | 206 x 177.5 x up to 117 | Upright, as exported. **Lightning infill** (or 5–8% gyroid), 3 walls, 5 top layers. No supports |
| `deck_girder_span_print_upside_down` | 12 | 146 x 27 x 17.6 | Deck on the bed. The bottom lateral bracing on top is a short bridge |
| `pier_full` | 4 | 44 x 104 x 46 | Upright. 45° chamfer under the coping |
| `pier_joint_half` | 2 | 22 x 104 x 46 | Upright. Module B uses the same part, turned 180° |
| `bowtie_key` | 28 | 32 x 16 x 5.6 | Flat |

Every tile fits a 220 x 220 bed (Ender 3, Prusa MK4, Bambu A1/P1/X1). Tiles are named by row (`front`/`back`) and
column (1 = transition end, 3 = joint end).

## Assembly
1. **Join the tiles:** dry-fit the 6 tiles of a module upside down on a flat table. Glue the seams (CA or plastic cement),
   then glue a bow-tie key into each pocket on the underside (14 per module) across the seams. Fill and sand the seams on top.
2. **Piers:** glue the piers into the sockets in the river bed. Glue the half pier into the open-ended socket at the joint end.
   Paint the piers and abutment concrete (light tan-grey), the rock grey and brown, and the river bed dark brown.
3. **Water:** tape a temporary dam across the river at the joint end, then pour 3 mm of resin or acrylic water.
   The low lip along the front edge holds it in.
4. **Girders:** paint them weathered steel brown, then set them on the piers and abutment seats. They sit 0.5 mm clear of the backwall
   and of each other. A dot of glue on each shoe holds them in place.
5. **Track:** lay Unitrack (248 + 248 + 124 per line) on the embankment and across the girder decks. It overhangs each module end by 1 mm.
6. **Scenery:** add foliage and trees on the cliff (commercial clump foliage and trees in autumn colours suit the look), and ballast or grass on the embankment.

## Checks
- All STLs are watertight. In the assembled model the girders, piers and terrain do not overlap; the girders sit on their
  bearing shoes on every pier and abutment seat, and the piers sit in their sockets.
- The tile seams miss every pier socket.
